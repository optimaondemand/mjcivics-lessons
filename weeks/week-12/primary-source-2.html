Second Amendment (1791)
Read this, then complete Primary Source Report 12 in Canvas.
📖 CONTEXT

The Second Amendment's single sentence has been read two ways for over two centuries: as protecting an individual right to keep and bear arms, or as protecting that right only in connection with organized militia service. Read it alongside Lesson 12.2's account of the historical context and the Supreme Court's eventual ruling in District of Columbia v. Heller (2008).

📜 THE TEXT
“A well regulated Militia, being necessary to the security of a free State, the right of the people to keep and bear Arms, shall not be infringed.”
(Second Amendment to the United States Constitution, ratified 1791.)
💡 A NOTE ON THE TEXT
The original enrolled 1791 text capitalizes "Militia," "State," and "Arms," a stylistic habit of the period rather than a signal of special legal emphasis. More significant is the sentence's structure itself: a prefatory clause ("A well regulated Militia, being necessary to the security of a free State") followed by an operative clause ("the right of the people to keep and bear Arms, shall not be infringed"). Whether the first clause limits the second is exactly the question Lesson 12.2 traces from the framers' own historical concerns through District of Columbia v. Heller.
