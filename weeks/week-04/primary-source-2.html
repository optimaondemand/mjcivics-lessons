Rousseau, On the Social Contract, Book I, Ch. 6

📖 CONTEXT

This is Rousseau's own description of the act of association that forms a political community, the moment individuals become, in his words, citizens sharing in the sovereign power. Read it alongside Lesson 4.2's account of the general will and how it differs from Locke's version of the social contract.

📜 THE TEXT
“Each of us puts his person and all his power in common under the supreme direction of the general will, and, in return, each is received as an indivisible part of the whole… At once, in place of the individual person of each contracting party, this act of association produces a moral and collective body… This public person, so formed by the union of all other persons, formerly took the name of city, and now takes that of Republic or body politic; it is called by its members State when passive, Sovereign when active, and Power when compared with others like itself. Those who are associated in it take collectively the name of people, and severally are called citizens, as sharing in the sovereign power.”
(Cole translation, standard public-domain text, condensed.)
