"Neither Wholly Federal Nor Wholly National"
Read this, then complete the Extension Assignment in Canvas.
📖 CONTEXT

This benchmark, on the advantages of a federal system, is not one of this week's official course-map primary sources; it is introduced here as enrichment because it comes from the same set of essays as this week's Federalist No. 51. Formal treatment of the standard is tracked separately in the Standards Crosswalk.

Critics of the proposed Constitution asked a sharp question: was the new government a single national government swallowing the states, or a league of independent states like the Articles of Confederation had been? In Federalist No. 39, Madison answered that it was neither: a deliberately mixed design historians now call federalism.

📜 THE TEXT
“The proposed Constitution, therefore, is, in strictness, neither a national nor a federal Constitution, but a composition of both. In its foundation it is federal, not national… in the sources from which the ordinary powers of government are drawn, it is partly federal and partly national… and, finally, in the authoritative mode of introducing amendments… it is neither wholly federal nor wholly national.”
(Condensed, public-domain text.)
