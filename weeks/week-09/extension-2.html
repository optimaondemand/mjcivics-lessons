📖 CONTEXT

Two short passages define how state and national power fit together: Article IV requires states to respect each other's laws and citizens, and the Tenth Amendment reserves to the states everything the Constitution did not specifically hand to the federal government.

This benchmark, on Article IV and the 10th Amendment, is not one of this week's official course-map primary sources; it is introduced here as enrichment. Formal treatment of the standard is tracked separately in the Standards Crosswalk.

📜 THE TEXT
“Full Faith and Credit shall be given in each State to the public Acts, Records, and judicial Proceedings of every other State… The Citizens of each State shall be entitled to all Privileges and Immunities of Citizens in the several States.” (Article IV, §§1–2)

“The powers not delegated to the United States by the Constitution, nor prohibited by it to the States, are reserved to the States respectively, or to the people.” (Amendment X)
