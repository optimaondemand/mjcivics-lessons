Primary Source 1
Brown v. Board of Education, 347 U.S. 483 (1954)
Read this, then complete Critical Questions 15.1 in Canvas.
📖 CONTEXT

Chief Justice Warren's opinion, joined by all nine Justices, held that racially segregated public schools violate the Fourteenth Amendment's guarantee of equal protection, regardless of whether physical resources were formally equal. Read it alongside Lesson 15.1's account of the sixty years of "separate but equal" doctrine this ruling overturned.

📜 THE TEXT
“To separate [children in grade and high schools] from others of similar age and qualifications solely because of their race generates a feeling of inferiority as to their status in the community that may affect their hearts and minds in a way unlikely ever to be undone… We conclude that in the field of public education the doctrine of ‘separate but equal’ has no place. Separate educational facilities are inherently unequal. Therefore, we hold that the plaintiffs and others similarly situated… are, by reason of the segregation complained of, deprived of the equal protection of the laws guaranteed by the Fourteenth Amendment.”
(Opinion of the Court, Chief Justice Warren, unanimous; condensed, public-domain text.)
💡 A NOTE ON THIS OPINION
Watch the word "inherently unequal": the Court is not saying segregated schools happened to be unequal in this one case because of unmatched funding. It is saying separation itself, no matter how carefully resources are matched, produces the harm. This is the exact claim Lesson 15.2's primary source, the 1896 Plessy v. Ferguson ruling, denied, and this excerpt is the Court's direct, fifty-eight-years-later answer to it.
