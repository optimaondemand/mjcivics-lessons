2.63 Primary Source 2
Aristotle, Politics, Book V, Ch. 1
Read this, then complete 2.64 Primary Source Report in Canvas.
📖 CONTEXT

Aristotle diagnoses the perception of inequality, not necessarily inequality of wealth alone, as the chief cause of revolution, and describes how this plays out differently in democracies and oligarchies. Read it alongside Lesson 8.2's account of how this diagnosis bears on Hamilton's electoral design.

📜 THE TEXT
“The universal and chief cause of this revolutionary feeling… is the desire of equality, when men think that they are equal to others who have more than themselves; or, again, the desire of inequality and superiority, when conceiving themselves to be superior they think that they have not more but the same or less than their inferiors… Revolutions in democracies are generally caused by the intemperance of demagogues, who either in their private capacity lay information against rich men… or… stir up the people against them; and in states also which are large… revolutions are more violent than in states in which the people have a smaller share of power… for the many, if they think that they are at a disadvantage, become factious.”
(Jowett translation, standard public-domain text, condensed.)
