📖 CONTEXT

This benchmark on the civic problem-solving process is not one of this week's official course-map primary sources; it is introduced here as enrichment because it sits so closely beside Week 3's account of the Olive Branch Petition and this week's Declaration. Formal treatment of the standard is tracked separately in the Standards Crosswalk.

Before declaring independence, the Continental Congress tried the formal channel available to them: a direct, respectful petition to King George III, known as the Olive Branch Petition (July 1775), the very appeal to the King against Parliament you read about in Week 3. It modeled a process still recognizable today: identify the authority that can actually address the problem, state the grievance clearly, and propose a specific resolution, even though the King ultimately refused to receive it.

📜 THE TEXT
“We therefore beseech your Majesty, that your royal authority and influence may be graciously interposed to procure us relief from our afflicting fears and jealousies… and that a happy and permanent reconciliation may be established between them, in the manner most acceptable to your Majesty.”
