The Constitution's Age Requirements
Read this, then complete the Extension Assignment in Canvas.
📖 CONTEXT

The Constitution sets specific, rising qualifications for each national office, age, citizenship, and residency requirements that get stricter the more power the office holds.

This benchmark, on qualifications to hold office, is not one of this week's official course-map primary sources; it is introduced here as enrichment, since it connects directly to this week's Article II reading on the presidency. Formal treatment of the standard is tracked separately in the Standards Crosswalk.

📜 THE TEXT
“No Person shall be a Representative who shall not have attained to the Age of twenty five Years, and been seven Years a Citizen of the United States, and who shall not, when elected, be an Inhabitant of that State in which he shall be chosen.” (Art. I, §2)

“No Person shall be a Senator who shall not have attained to the Age of thirty Years, and been nine Years a Citizen of the United States, and who shall not, when elected, be an Inhabitant of that State for which he shall be chosen.” (Art. I, §3)

“No Person except a natural born Citizen… shall be eligible to the Office of President; neither shall any Person be eligible to that Office who shall not have attained to the Age of thirty five Years, and been fourteen Years a Resident within the United States.” (Art. II, §1)
