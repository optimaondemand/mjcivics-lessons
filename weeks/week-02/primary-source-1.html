Cicero, De Re Publica, Book I
Read this, then complete 1.17 Critical Questions in Canvas.
📖 CONTEXT

Cicero, writing as the Republic he admired was already unraveling, offers a definition of the res publica, the commonwealth, that grounds legitimate government not in force but in shared agreement about justice and the common good. Read it alongside Lesson 2.1's account of the Consuls, the Senate, and the Assemblies.

📜 THE TEXT
“A commonwealth [res publica] is the property of a people [res populi]. But a people is not every group of men, however assembled, but an assemblage of people in large numbers associated in an agreement with respect to justice and a partnership for the common good… The cause which brings about this association is not so much human weakness as a certain social spirit which nature has implanted in man… For man is not a solitary or unsocial creature, but born with such a nature that not even under conditions of great prosperity of every sort is he able to isolate himself.”
(Sabine and Smith translation, condensed.)
