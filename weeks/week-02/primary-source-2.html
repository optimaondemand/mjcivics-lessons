📖 CONTEXT

Polybius, the Greek historian brought to Rome as a hostage, argues that the Roman constitution genuinely defies simple classification: depending on which institution you examine, it looks monarchic, aristocratic, or democratic. Read it alongside Lesson 2.2's account of the Consuls, the Senate, and the Assemblies.

📜 THE TEXT
“For if one fixed one's eyes on the power of the consuls, the constitution seemed completely monarchical and royal; if on that of the senate, it seemed again aristocratic; and when one looked at the power of the masses, it seemed clearly to be a democracy. The type of government was in fact such that… it was impossible even for a native to pronounce with certainty whether the whole system was aristocratic, democratic, or monarchical. And no wonder: for if we confine our attention to the power of the consuls we should be inclined to regard it as entirely monarchical… if again we fix our attention on the senate it seems aristocratic; and if finally one looks at the power possessed by the people it seems a clear case of a democratic government.”
(Paton translation, Loeb Classical Library, condensed.)
